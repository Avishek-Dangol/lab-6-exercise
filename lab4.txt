Answer: Every pixel has to finish its mandelbrotSmooth call before the next frame can be drawn, so a high iteration count 
while you're panning or zooming would make the app feel frozen. Starting at 50 means you get a rough but immediate picture
as you move, and the detail fills in by doubling once you stop, which feels much smoother than staring at a stuck window.

Answer: Since no pixel depends on any other, one main thread can split the image into chunks, such as horizontal strips or
tiles, hand them to worker threads, and wait for them all to finish before stitching the result together. Small tiles
work better than one big strip per worker, because the black regions near the set take far longer to compute than the fast-escaping
outside, and with small tiles an idle worker can just grab the next one instead of waiting on a slow neighbour.

Answer:Visually, averaging the colours of four sub-pixel samples would smooth out the jagged edges and turn the speckled noise 
into a softer, more accurate blend of what's actually inside that pixel, so the image looks cleaner and less grainy. The catch 
is that it costs about four times as much work per frame, which hurts most at high iteration counts, so you'd probably only use 
it once the view has settled and you have time to spare.
